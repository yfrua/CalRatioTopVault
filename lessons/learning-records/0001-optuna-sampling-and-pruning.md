# Optuna TPE Sampling and Multi-Objective Cohort Pruning Established

Established the conceptual and algorithmic foundation of Optuna's Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE), specifically how it partitions trial histories into good ($\ell(x)$) and poor ($g(x)$) non-parametric densities to maximize Expected Improvement via $\ell(x)/g(x)$. Analyzed the mechanics of Step 4 (Density Estimation), clarifying how historical points dictate kernel means, nearest-neighbor spacing governs adaptive bandwidths ($\sigma_i$), recency weighting prioritizes recent trials, and global priors safeguard against zero-density starvation. Resolved the question of how 2D multi-objective TPE (MOTPE) handles partitioning without a scalar threshold $y^*$, establishing that the quantile $\gamma$ determines the quota $n_{\text{below}}$, non-dominated Pareto ranking selects candidate layers, and Hypervolume Subset Selection (HSSP) breaks rank boundary ties to maximize 2D hypervolume, with trials weighted by marginal hypervolume contribution. Confirmed that standard Optuna pruners cannot operate on multi-objective Pareto metrics, establishing why this repository uses a custom Euclidean trajectory pruner (`_CohortTrajectoryPruner`) matching PyTorch Lightning checkpoint callbacks, enhanced with patient scale-factor aware warmup ($\max(\text{base}, \text{sf\_pct\_end})$) to ensure complementary training engages fully before pruning decisions.
